How we think about gathering

Three ideas that change everything

Hosting should be this easy +

You're not planning a dinner party. You're picking an activity that does the social heavy lifting, pressing a button, and showing up. A pottery class, a walk, a grocery run — the experience carries the weight so you don't have to.

Go with most, not all +

Stop waiting for the perfect date. It doesn't exist. The time that works for most people wins. Host again for those who missed it — or better yet, let them host the next one.

Keep showing up +

One casual walk every week builds more than one ambitious dinner party every quarter. Make gathering a habit, not an occasion. Frequency beats intensity.

Four Ways to Gather

Connection happens through proximity, intention, and the people your people know.

Village Mode
The people already near you — your neighbors, your building, your block. A walk, a grocery run, a coffee. Doing mundane things together is what turns strangers into familiar faces and familiar faces into a village.
Discovery Mode
Your friends know incredible people you haven't met yet. Everyone brings someone new — a warm introduction over a shared experience. No profiles, no swiping. Meeting new people will only ever add to your life.
Kindred Mode
People you've met and felt that spark of recognition — "this person gets it." Invest in those connections through repeated, shared experiences. These are the people who could still be in your life ten years from now.
Perennial Mode
Your people. You know it, they know it. But life gets busy and suddenly it's been four months. Perennial mode makes it effortless to keep showing up for the ones who matter most.
